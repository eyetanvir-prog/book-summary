On War

Clausewitz’s unfinished treatise on war remains the reference point for anyone thinking seriously about force and politics.

Carl von ClausewitzFirst published 1835About 461 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    War is the continuation of politics by other means, so military action only makes sense when tied to a political purpose.

  2. 02

    Friction and the fog of war mean plans meet confusion, fatigue, and chance, which is why judgment matters more than formulas.

  3. 03

    He describes a shifting relationship between passion, chance, and reason — people, army, and government — that every strategy must balance.

Open Library note. Vom Kriege is the basic work on the Western way of war. For Clauswitz, war is "politics by other means". It concentrates on defeating the enemy by massing overwhelming force at the enemy's weak point. In his rationalistic model, the prince (or other governing elite) begins with a set of objectives and chooses the most cost-effective way of accomplishing them. If force is seen as that way, then the prince chooses war.
