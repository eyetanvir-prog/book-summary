Good to Great

Jim Collins studies companies that jumped from average to outstanding results and asks what they did differently from close rivals.

Collins, James C.First published 2001About 300 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The breakthrough firms had humble, fiercely determined “Level 5” leaders who put the company’s success above their own reputations.

  2. 02

    Get the right people on the bus before choosing direction, then focus on the hedgehog concept: what you can be best at, love, and profit from.

  3. 03

    Several featured companies later stumbled badly, a reminder that retrospective case studies can mistake luck and survivorship for durable principles.
