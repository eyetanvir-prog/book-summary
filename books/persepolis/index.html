Persepolis

Marjane Satrapi’s graphic memoir of growing up during Iran’s revolution and war, drawn in stark black and white.

Marjane SatrapiFirst published 2000About 352 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    A child’s perspective reveals how politics enters homes, schools, clothing, and friendships under revolutionary rule.

  2. 02

    Satrapi shows contradictions between public restrictions and private family life, where debate and humor persist.

  3. 03

    The simple drawings make violence and repression easier to approach while sharpening their emotional impact.
