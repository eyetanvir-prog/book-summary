A Time to Kill

In 1980s Mississippi, a Black father kills the men who raped his daughter, and a young lawyer takes his defense.

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Grisham uses the trial to examine racial justice in the South, asking whether a Black defendant can get a fair hearing.

  2. 02

    Jake Brigance faces threats, financial ruin, and moral doubt, showing what principled advocacy can cost.

  3. 03

    Its depiction of race is earnest yet dated in places, and readers today may note who gets centered in the story.

Open Library note. A Time to Kill is a 1989 legal thriller and debut novel by American author John Grisham. The novel was rejected by many publishers before Wynwood Press eventually gave it a 5,000-copy printing. When Doubleday published The Firm, Wynwood released a trade paperback of A Time to Kill, which became a bestseller. Dell published the mass market paperback months after the success of The Firm, bringing Grisham to widespread popularity among readers.
