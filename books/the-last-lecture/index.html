The Last Lecture

A computer scientist with terminal cancer writes down what he wants his young children to know about living well.

Randy PauschFirst published 2008About 233 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Pausch frames childhood dreams as a practical guide: chasing them teaches persistence, and helping other people reach theirs turns out to be the bigger reward.

  2. 02

    Brick walls, in his telling, exist to show how badly you want something; they keep out the people who don’t want it enough.

  3. 03

    The book’s force comes from its restraint: it is cheerful, specific, and unsentimental, which makes the underlying grief land harder than any lament would.
