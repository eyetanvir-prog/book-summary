The Perfect Storm

A swordfishing boat out of Gloucester meets a rare collision of weather systems in the North Atlantic in 1991.

Sebastian JungerFirst published 1997About 256 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Junger explains how three systems combined to produce enormous waves, giving a clear lesson in how storms actually form.

  2. 02

    The book portrays commercial fishing as dangerous, poorly paid work where money pressures push crews out despite risk.

  3. 03

    Since no one knows exactly what happened aboard, Junger reconstructs it carefully from evidence, and is honest about that limit.

Open Library note. It was the storm of the century, boasting waves over one hundred feet high --- a tempest created by so rare a combination of factors that meteorologists deemed it "the perfect storm." When it struck in October 1991, there was a virtually no warning. "She's comin; on, boys, and she's comin' on strong." radioed Captain Billy Tyne of the Andrew Gail off the coast of Nova Scotia, and soon afterward the boat and its crew of six disappeared without a trace.
