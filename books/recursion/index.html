A New York cop and a neuroscientist chase a technology that lets people revisit memories, and the past stops staying put.

Blake CrouchFirst published 2019About 352 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Crouch asks what would happen if memory were editable, and the answer is that identity, grief and reality itself become negotiable.

  2. 02

    The book moves at screenplay speed, trading depth of character for big structural swings that fold the timeline back on itself.

  3. 03

    Beneath the high concept sits a simple moral argument: the pain of what happened is part of who we are, and erasing it has a price.
