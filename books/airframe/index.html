Three ideas to keep

    Crichton turns accident investigation into suspense, showing how engineers sift data, parts, and testimony to explain a failure.

  2. 02

    His biggest target is television news, portrayed as eager to simplify complex technical stories for drama.

  3. 03

    It offers useful insight into aviation safety culture, though its media critique is blunt and one-sided.

Open Library note. Airframe is a novel by the American writer Michael Crichton, his eleventh under his own name and twenty-first overall, first published in 1996, in hardcover, by Knopf and then in 1997, as a paperback, by Ballantine Books. The plot follows Casey Singleton, a quality assurance vice president at the fictional aerospace manufacturer Norton Aircraft, as she investigates an in-flight accident aboard a Norton-manufactured airliner that leaves three passengers dead and 56 injured.
