David and Goliath

Malcolm Gladwell argues that apparent disadvantages can be strengths, and that underdogs often win more than expected.

Malcolm GladwellFirst published 2013About 305 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Gladwell argues that powerful advantages can become weaknesses at the extremes, and apparent weaknesses can force useful adaptations.

  2. 02

    He uses stories from education, sports, and civil rights to suggest that difficulty can sometimes build resilience.

  3. 03

    Critics argue his anecdotes overreach the research, so treat his conclusions as interesting hypotheses rather than settled findings.
