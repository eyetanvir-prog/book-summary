I Am Malala

A Pakistani schoolgirl speaks out for education under Taliban threat, survives an assassination attempt, and keeps speaking.

Malala YousafzaiFirst published 2012About 336 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Education is the book’s central demand: girls’ schooling is framed as both a personal right and a social necessity.

  2. 02

    Malala’s family and community matter; her father’s activism and school helped shape her public courage.

  3. 03

    The memoir shows how extremism grows through fear, propaganda, and weak institutions, not just through violence.

Open Library note. When the Taliban took control of the Swat Valley in Pakistan, one girl spoke out. Malala Yousafzai refused to be silenced and fought for her right to an education. On Tuesday, October 9, 2012, when she was fifteen, she almost paid the ultimate price. She was shot in the head at point-blank range while riding the bus home from school, and few expected her to survive.
