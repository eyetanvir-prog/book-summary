Why I'm No Longer Talking to White People About Race

A British journalist expands a viral blog post into a clear-eyed account of structural racism in the United Kingdom.

Reni Eddo-LodgeFirst published 2017About 256 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Britain has its own long history of race, slavery, and empire, and treating racism as an American import lets institutions avoid examining themselves.

  2. 02

    Eddo-Lodge argues that structural racism lives in outcomes — schooling, policing, hiring — not just in individual malice, so good intentions are not enough.

  3. 03

    White privilege, she writes, is often the absence of a burden, which is why it can feel invisible to those who hold it.

Open Library note. In 2014, award-winning journalist Reni Eddo-Lodge wrote on her blog about her frustration with the way that discussions of race and racism in Britain were being led by those who weren't affected by it. Her words hit a nerve. The post went viral and comments flooded in from others desperate to speak up about their own experiences. Galvanised, she decided to dig into the source of these feelings.
