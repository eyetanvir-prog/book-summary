Hayy ibn Yaqzan

Ibn Tufayl’s philosophical tale of a child raised alone on an island who reasons his way to knowledge of God.

Ibn TufaylFirst published 2009About 280 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The story explores whether human reason, unaided by society or revelation, can discover truth about nature and the divine.

  2. 02

    Hayy’s stages of discovery — from observation to philosophy to mystical experience — map a medieval theory of knowledge.

  3. 03

    Its later encounter with organized religion raises questions about how philosophy and popular faith relate, still debated today.

Open Library note. "In this classic of medieval Islamic philosophy, Ibn Tufayl tells of a child raised by a doe on an equatorial island who grows up to discover the world and his own place in it, unaided - but also unimpeded - by society, language or tradition. Hayy's encounters with nature, God, and man challenge the values of the culture in which the tale was written as well as those of contemporary society."--Back cover.
