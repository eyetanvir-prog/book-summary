Mexican Gothic

A 1950s socialite visits an English-run mansion in the Mexican hills and finds the old gothic house rotten to the core.

Silvia Moreno-GarciaFirst published 2020About 352 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Moreno-Garcia uses the classic gothic kit — isolated house, sick cousin, sinister family — to talk about colonialism, eugenics and extraction.

  2. 02

    Noemí is not a fainting heroine; her vanity and stubbornness become the tools she uses to read a household designed to make her doubt herself.

  3. 03

    The slow-burn first half is atmosphere and manners, then the horror turns bodily and strange; patience is part of the price of admission.

Open Library note. An isolated mansion. A chillingly charismatic aristocrat. And a brave socialite drawn to expose their treacherous secrets. . . . From the author of Gods of Jade and Shadow comes “a terrifying twist on classic gothic horror” (Kirkus Reviews) set in glamorous 1950s Mexico. After receiving a frantic letter from her newly-wed cousin begging for someone to save her from a mysterious doom, Noemí Taboada heads to High Place, a distant house in the Mexican countryside.
