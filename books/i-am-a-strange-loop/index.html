    Hofstadter suggests that the sense of being an I arises from self-referential loops in the brain, not a hidden inner essence.

    Because selves are patterns, parts of a person can live on in others who deeply internalise them.

Open Library note. Hofstadter's long-awaited return to the themes of Gödel, Escher, Bach--an original and controversial view of the nature of consciousness and identity. What do we mean when we say "I"? Can a self, a soul, a consciousness, an "I" arise out of mere matter? If it cannot, then how can you or I be here? This book argues that the key to understanding selves and consciousness is a special kind of abstract feedback loop inhabiting our brains.
