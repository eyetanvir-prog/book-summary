The Audacity of Hope

Then-Senator Barack Obama’s reflections on American politics, values, and how to move beyond bitter partisanship.

Barack ObamaFirst published 2006About 413 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Obama argues Americans share more common values than partisan politics suggests, and that compromise is a democratic necessity.

  2. 02

    He describes how fundraising, media, and fear of losing distort politics, pushing even well-meaning politicians toward caution.

  3. 03

    Chapters on faith, race, family, and the Constitution blend policy argument with personal reflection, previewing his later presidential themes.

Open Library note. Senator Obama calls for a different brand of politics--a politics for those weary of bitter partisanship and alienated by the "endless clash of armies" we see in Congress and on the campaign trail; a politics rooted in the faith, inclusiveness, and nobility of spirit at the heart of our democracy. He explores those forces--from the fear of losing, to the perpetual need to raise money, to the power of the media--that can stifle even the best-intentioned politician.
