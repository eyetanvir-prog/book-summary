Mindhunter

An FBI profiler’s memoir about learning to read crime scenes by interviewing the killers who made them.

John E. DouglasFirst published 1995About 397 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Douglas describes building criminal profiling by sitting down with convicted murderers and asking how and why they chose victims and methods.

  2. 02

    The core tool is behaviour as evidence: what an offender does at a scene reveals needs and habits that can narrow an investigation.

  3. 03

    He is candid about the job’s toll on his health and family, though some of profiling’s reliability claims are more contested than the book suggests.
