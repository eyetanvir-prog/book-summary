Children of Dune

Herbert keeps dismantling the hero myth he built, following Paul Atreides’s twin children as they inherit an empire and a terrible foresight.

Frank HerbertFirst published 1976About 504 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Prophecy is treated as a trap: seeing the future narrows your choices until the only escape may require giving up your humanity.

  2. 02

    The book shows a religion calcifying around a dead leader, with priests, bureaucrats and pilgrims turning a living movement into an instrument of control.

  3. 03

    Arrakis itself is changing as it turns green, and Herbert uses that ecology to ask whether a people can survive losing the hardship that defined them.
