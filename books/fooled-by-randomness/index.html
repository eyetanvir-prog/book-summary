Fooled by Randomness

Nassim Taleb argues that people routinely mistake luck for skill, especially in markets and careers.

Nassim Nicholas TalebFirst published 2001About 312 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Survivorship bias makes winners look skilled when many equally talented people failed from bad luck alone.

  2. 02

    Judge decisions by the full range of possible outcomes, not just what happened, because a bad bet can still pay off.

  3. 03

    Taleb’s combative style can grate, but his warning about randomness and hidden risk remains widely useful.
