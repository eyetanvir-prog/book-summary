The House in the Cerulean Sea

A rule-following caseworker inspects an island orphanage for magical children and slowly discovers his own life has been too small.

T. J. KluneFirst published 2019About 416 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The book is openly a comfort read about found family, prejudice and the courage it takes to stop obeying rules that harm people.

  2. 02

    Bureaucracy is the villain, and Klune mocks systems that classify and monitor children instead of caring for them.

  3. 03

    Its gentleness is the point; readers who want moral complexity may find it sweet and simple, while others will treasure exactly that.
