Assassin's Apprentice

A royal bastard is trained as a court assassin, and Hobb makes his loneliness and divided loyalty the real story.

Robin HobbFirst published 1995About 400 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The fantasy is unusually interior, told in a reflective first person that dwells on belonging, shame and the cost of serving powerful people.

  2. 02

    Magic comes with social stigma and bodily risk, so abilities feel like burdens that shape relationships rather than simple tools for winning.

  3. 03

    Hobb takes her time building court politics and character, rewarding patient readers who want emotional depth more than constant action.

Open Library note. Young Fitz is the bastard son of the noble Prince Chivalry, raised in the shadow of the royal court by his father's gruff stableman. He is treated like an outcast by all the royalty except the devious King Shrewd, who has him secretly tutored in the arts of the assassin. For in Fitz's blood runs the magic Skill--and the darker knowledge of a child raised with the stable hounds and rejected by his family. As barbarous raiders ravage the coasts, Fitz is growing to manhood.
