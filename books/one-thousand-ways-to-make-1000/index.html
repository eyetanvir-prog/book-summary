One Thousand Ways to Make $1000

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Ordinary people built income from modest starts, showing that a small idea pursued with effort can grow into a real business.

  2. 02

    Success often came from noticing a local need and serving it well, rather than from capital, credentials, or invention.

  3. 03

    Many specific schemes are dated, but the underlying lessons about thrift, initiative, and compounding small gains remain useful.

Open Library note. First published in 1936, One Thousand Ways to Make $1000 is the long out-of-print book that Warren Buffett's biographers credit with shaping the legendary investor's business acumen and giving him his trademark appreciation of compound interest. After pulling a copy of One Thousand Ways off a library shelf at age eleven and devouring F.C. Minaker's plucky and practical business advice, Buffett declared that he would be a millionaire by the time he was 35.
