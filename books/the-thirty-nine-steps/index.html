The Thirty-Nine Steps

A bored colonial returnee finds a murdered stranger in his London flat and goes on the run across Scotland.

John BuchanFirst published 1915About 116 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Buchan helped invent the man-on-the-run thriller: an ordinary, resourceful hero framed and chased by police and enemy agents alike.

  2. 02

    The pleasures are speed and landscape, with Hannay improvising disguises and escapes across moors described by a writer who loved them.

  3. 03

    It carries the prejudices of its 1915 moment, so read it as a brisk historical artifact as much as an adventure.

Open Library note. Richard Hannay has just returned to England after years in South Africa and is thoroughly bored with his life in London. But then a murder is committed in his flat, just days after a chance encounter with an American who had told him about an assassination plot which could have dire international consequences. An obvious suspect for the police and an easy target for the killers, Hannay goes on the run in his native Scotland where he will need all his courage and ingenuity to stay one step ahead of his pursuers.
