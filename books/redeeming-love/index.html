Redeeming Love

The biblical story of Hosea retold in the California gold rush, centred on grace offered to a deeply wounded woman.

Francine RiversFirst published 1991About 474 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Rivers frames unconditional love as a mirror of divine grace, arguing that healing begins when someone refuses to give up on you.

  2. 02

    Angel’s distrust is shaped by childhood abuse, and the book shows recovery as slow and repeatedly interrupted.

  3. 03

    It is beloved in Christian fiction, though some readers find its portrayal of trauma and gender roles uncomfortable.

Open Library note. California’s gold country, 1850. A time when men sold their souls for a bag of gold and women sold their bodies for a place to sleep. Angel expects nothing from men but betrayal. Sold into prostitution as a child, she survives by keeping her hatred alive. And what she hates most are the men who use her, leaving her empty and dead inside. Then she meets Michael Hosea. A man who seeks his Father’s heart in everything, Michael Hosea obeys God’s call to marry Angel and to love her unconditionally.
