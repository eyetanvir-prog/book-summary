Mrs. Dalloway

One June day in 1920s London, a society hostess prepares for a party while a shell-shocked veteran moves through the same city.

Virginia WoolfFirst published 1925About 224 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Woolf’s stream of consciousness shows how memory, regret and observation flow together in a single ordinary day.

  2. 02

    Clarissa and Septimus never meet, yet the novel connects them, setting private reflection against postwar trauma.

  3. 03

    The book is about the choices that shape a life — marriage, love, independence — and how they are revisited in passing moments.

Open Library note. Virginia Woolf’s novel chronicles a day in the life of Clarissa Dalloway, a politician’s wife in 1920s London, as she prepares to host a party that evening. The narrative follows Clarissa’s thoughts (and sometimes those of people she meets) as she goes about her errands, and events in the day remind her of her youth and friendships from the past. As the book progresses characters from the past emerge, igniting old feelings and making Clarissa question the life she has created for herself. Mrs.
