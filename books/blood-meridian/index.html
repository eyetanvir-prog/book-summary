Blood Meridian

A teenage drifter rides with scalp hunters along the US–Mexico border, in prose as beautiful as the violence is unbearable.

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    McCarthy strips the Western of heroism, showing westward expansion as organised slaughter driven by greed and contempt.

  2. 02

    Judge Holden, a learned and monstrous figure, embodies the claim that war and domination are natural human law.

  3. 03

    The book is hard to read for its relentless violence and biblical style, but it rewards attention as a meditation on evil.

Open Library note. An epic novel of the violence and depravity that attended America's westward expansion, Blood Meridian brilliantly subverts the conventions of the Western novel and the mythology of the "wild west." Based on historical events that took place on the Texas-Mexico border in the 1850s, it traces the fortunes of the Kid, a fourteen-year-old Tennesseean who stumbles into the nightmarish world where Indians are being murdered and the market for their scalps is thriving.
