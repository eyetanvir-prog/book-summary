Murder on the Orient Express

A snowdrift stops a luxury train, a passenger is found stabbed, and Poirot has a carriage full of suspects with perfect alibis.

Agatha ChristieFirst published 1933About 240 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The locked setting turns detection into pure interviewing, and Poirot’s method is to notice which stories fit together a little too neatly.

  2. 02

    Every passenger seems to be playing a type, and the pleasure lies in watching Christie use those types to hide what is really going on.

  3. 03

    Its ending raises a real moral question about law versus justice, and Poirot has to decide which one he actually serves.

Open Library note. While en route from Syria to Paris, in the middle of a freezing winter's night, the Orient Express is stopped dead in its tracks by a snowdrift. Passengers awake to find the train still stranded and to discover that a wealthy American has been brutally stabbed to death in his private compartment. Incredibly, that compartment is locked from the inside. With no escape into the wintery landscape the killer must still be on board.
