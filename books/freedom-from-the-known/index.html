Freedom from the Known

Krishnamurti urges readers to see through inherited beliefs and find freedom through direct, honest observation.

Jiddu KrishnamurtiFirst published 1959About 128 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Krishnamurti argues that dependence on authority, tradition, or teachers prevents real understanding of ourselves.

  2. 02

    Fear arises from thought projecting into the future, and seeing this process clearly can dissolve it.

  3. 03

    He offers no method, insisting that freedom comes from attention rather than following a system.

Open Library note. A masterpiece from Jiddu Krishnamurti on freedom. The author says about the chains in which the present man live, about his fears of the known and fear of the unknown. He employs a witty sarcasm at the way in which people are ready to accept the ideological tyranny of religious institutions when they are not ready to accept any kind of political dictatorship. Freedom from all the shackles that draw us backward from being a universal citizen - that is the core topic discussed in this book.
