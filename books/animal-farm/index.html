Animal Farm

A short fable about farm animals overthrowing their owner that becomes a precise satire of how revolutions betray themselves.

George OrwellFirst published 1945About 128 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Orwell targets Stalinism specifically, but the pattern — slogans bent, history rewritten, leaders turning into the old masters — is general.

  2. 02

    Propaganda does as much work as violence, with the pigs managing language and memory as carefully as they manage food.

  3. 03

    Its simplicity is the point; a child can follow the story, and an adult can see every political move it mirrors.

Open Library note. Animal Farm is a brilliant political satire and a powerful and affecting story of revolutions and idealism, power and corruption. 'All animals are equal. But some animals are more equal than others.' Mr Jones of Manor Farm is so lazy and drunken that one day he forgets to feed his livestock. The ensuing rebellion under the leadership of the pigs Napoleon and Snowball leads to the animals taking over the farm.
