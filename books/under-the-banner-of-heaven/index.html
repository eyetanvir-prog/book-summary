Under the Banner of Heaven

Krakauer uses a double murder by Mormon fundamentalists to examine faith, revelation, and violence in American religion.

Jon KrakauerFirst published 2003About 399 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The book traces how belief in personal revelation can be used to justify crimes when a believer says God commanded them.

  2. 02

    Krakauer intercuts the murder case with early Latter-day Saint history, including polygamy and frontier violence.

  3. 03

    The LDS Church objected strongly to its portrayal; read it as a sharp outsider’s argument about extremism, not a neutral history.

Open Library note. Jon Krakauer's literary reputation rests on insightful chronicles of lives conducted at the outer limits. In UNDER THE BANNER OF HEAVEN, he shifts his focus from extremes of physical adventure to extremes of religious belief within our own borders. At the core of his book is an appalling double murder committed by two Mormon fundamentalist brothers, Ron and Dan Lafferty, who insist they received a revelation from God commanding them to kill their blameless victims.
