Of Mice and Men

Two migrant workers in Depression-era California share a dream of their own land, and a short book shows how fragile that is.

John SteinbeckFirst published 1937About 119 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Steinbeck makes friendship the central value, in a world where most workers drift alone and trust no one.

  2. 02

    The ranch is full of people sidelined by age, race, disability or gender, each one lonely in a different way.

  3. 03

    The ending is quiet and devastating; it is worth reading without knowing anything more.

Open Library note. The second book in John Steinbeck’s labor trilogy, Of Mice and Men is a touching tale of two migrant laborers in search of work and eventual liberation from their social circumstances. Fiercely devoted to one another, George and Lennie plan to save up to finance their dream of someday owning a small piece of land. The pair seems unstoppable until tragedy strikes and their hopes come crashing down, forcing George to make a difficult decision regarding the welfare of his best friend.
