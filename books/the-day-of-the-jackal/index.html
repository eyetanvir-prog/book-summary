The Day of the Jackal

An anonymous professional assassin is hired to kill Charles de Gaulle, and a quiet detective has to work out who he is.

Frederick ForsythFirst published 1971About 416 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Forsyth writes procedure as suspense, showing forged papers, custom weapons, and patient police work in convincing step-by-step detail.

  2. 02

    You know the real-world outcome going in, yet the book stays tense because the question becomes how close it gets.

  3. 03

    It is a duel between two methodical professionals, neither flashy, and the satisfaction is watching competence meet competence.
