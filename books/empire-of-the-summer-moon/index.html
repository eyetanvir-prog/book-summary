Empire of the Summer Moon

A history of the Comanche nation’s long dominance of the southern plains, told through Quanah Parker and his captive-born mother.

S. C. GwynneFirst published 2010About 386 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The horse turned the Comanches into the most formidable mounted fighting force in North America, and their power shaped Spanish, Mexican, and Texan frontiers for generations.

  2. 02

    Cynthia Ann Parker’s life, taken as a child and later forcibly returned, shows how identity and belonging were contested on both sides of the frontier.

  3. 03

    Gwynne writes vividly and does not sanitize violence by anyone, though some readers find his framing of Comanche culture too fixated on brutality.
