Team of Rivals

How Lincoln filled his cabinet with the men who had tried to beat him, and made that awkward room work.

Doris Kearns GoodwinFirst published 2003About 916 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Lincoln chose former rivals Seward, Chase, and Bates because their talent mattered more to him than their loyalty or their opinion of him.

  2. 02

    His real skill was emotional management: absorbing slights, delaying anger, sharing credit, and taking blame so subordinates could keep doing their jobs.

  3. 03

    Goodwin’s group-portrait method shows leadership as a set of relationships over time, though some historians think she flatters Lincoln’s magnanimity a little.

Open Library note. Team of Rivals: The Political Genius of Abraham Lincoln is a 2005 book by Pulitzer Prize-winning American historian Doris Kearns Goodwin, published by Simon & Schuster. The book is a biographical portrait of U.S. President Abraham Lincoln and some of the men who served with him in his cabinet from 1861 to 1865. Three of his Cabinet members had previously run against Lincoln in the 1860 election: Attorney General Edward Bates, Secretary of the Treasury Salmon P. Chase and Secretary of State William H. Seward.
