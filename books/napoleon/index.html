Napoleon: A Life

A large, admiring biography that treats Napoleon as a lawmaker and administrator as much as a conqueror.

Andrew RobertsFirst published 2014About 926 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Roberts emphasises Napoleon’s civil legacy, the legal code, meritocratic administration, and modernised institutions that outlasted his empire.

  2. 02

    Using Napoleon’s vast correspondence, he shows a mind of extraordinary energy and attention to detail, alongside vanity and a growing appetite for risk.

  3. 03

    Critics find the portrait too sympathetic about the human cost of his wars, so weigh the admiration against the casualty lists.

Open Library note. Austerlitz, Borodino, Waterloo: his battles are among the greatest in history, but Napoleon Bonaparte was far more than a military genius and astute leader of men. Like George Washington and his own hero Julius Caesar, he was one of the greatest soldier-statesmen of all times. Andrew Roberts's Napoleon is the first one-volume biography to take advantage of the recent publication of Napoleon's thirty-three thousand letters, which radically transform our understanding of his character and motivation.
