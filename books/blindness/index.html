Blindness

A sudden epidemic of white blindness strips a city of its order, and Saramago records what people do in the dark.

José SaramagoFirst published 1995About 352 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Quarantine becomes a brutal social experiment, showing how quickly institutions and decency fail when people can no longer be seen or held accountable.

  2. 02

    Saramago leaves characters unnamed and runs dialogue together in long sentences, forcing you to read slowly and attend to voice instead of labels.

  3. 03

    The novel treats blindness as moral as well as physical, asking what it means to truly see other people and take responsibility for them.
