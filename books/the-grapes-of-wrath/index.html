The Grapes of Wrath

An Oklahoma family loses its farm in the Dust Bowl and heads to California, where the promised work turns into exploitation.

John SteinbeckFirst published 1939About 544 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Steinbeck alternates family chapters with wide-angle interludes, so one family stands for hundreds of thousands of displaced people.

  2. 02

    The book’s argument is that survival depends on solidarity, as the Joads learn to see themselves as part of a larger ‘we’.

  3. 03

    It is angry about corporate farming and the treatment of migrants, which made it controversial and still gives it bite.

Open Library note. Steinbeck’s classic novel of the Great Depression is as vivid now as ever. The story focuses on a family of Oklahoma sharecroppers, farmers who work another man’s land for a share of the crops. Driven from their home by drought and poverty they take to the road in a battered old truck and make their way to California to look for work. When they arrive they find hundreds of others like them being forced to work for breadline wages.
