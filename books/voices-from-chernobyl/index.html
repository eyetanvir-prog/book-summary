Voices from Chernobyl

Alexievich gathers testimonies from people who lived through Chernobyl, letting their voices tell the disaster.

Svetlana AlexievichFirst published 1997About 304 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The book is built from interviews, a chorus of widows, workers, scientists, and children, arranged with minimal commentary.

  2. 02

    It shows the moral and emotional disaster — grief, guilt, state lies — as much as the physical contamination.

  3. 03

    Alexievich captures a whole society facing something it had no words for, which makes the voices feel unfiltered and raw.
