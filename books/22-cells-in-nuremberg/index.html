22 Cells in Nuremberg

The American psychiatrist who examined Göring and other Nazi leaders before trial reports what he found in their minds.

Douglas McGlashan KelleyFirst published 1947About 245 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Kelley concluded that most of the defendants were not insane, a finding that unsettled readers hoping evil came from madness.

  2. 02

    He describes each prisoner through interviews and tests, showing ambition, vanity, and loyalty more than obvious monstrousness.

  3. 03

    His warning was that similar personalities exist in any country, so democracies must guard against the conditions that empower them.

Open Library note. 22 Cells in Nuremberg (1947) is a first-hand account by Dr. Douglas M. Kelley, the American psychiatrist tasked with evaluating 22 top-ranking Nazi leaders including Hermann Göring -- awaiting trial. Kelley provides psychological profiles and in-cell interviews, concluding that these leaders were largely sane, ordinary, and opportunistic, rather than unique monsters
