Push

A sixteen-year-old in Harlem, abused and written off by everyone around her, begins to claim a voice through learning to read.

SapphireFirst published 1996About 176 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Precious’s raw first-person narration shows how illiteracy and abuse combine to make someone invisible to every institution meant to help.

  2. 02

    An alternative school and a committed teacher prove that writing can be a lifeline, not a luxury, for people never allowed to speak.

  3. 03

    It is deliberately unflinching about sexual and physical abuse, so readers should prepare for difficult, graphic material.

Open Library note. Precious Jones, an illiterate sixteen-year-old, has up until now been invisible: invisible to the father who rapes her and the mother who batters her and to the authorities who dismiss her as just one more of Harlem's casualties. But when Precious, pregnant with a second child by her father, meets a determined and highly radical teacher, we follow her on a journey of education and enlightenment as Precious learns not only how to write about her life, but how to make it her own for the first time
