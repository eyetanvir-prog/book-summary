Musicophilia

Oliver Sacks collects patients whose brains do strange things with music, revealing how deeply sound is wired into us.

Oliver SacksFirst published 2007About 425 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Music survives damage that wipes out other abilities; people with severe amnesia or dementia can often still sing, play, or be steadied by familiar songs.

  2. 02

    Conditions like earworms, musical hallucinations, and sudden musical obsession show that the brain’s music systems can misfire in specific, studyable ways.

  3. 03

    Sacks writes as a clinician who sees people rather than cases, so the book reads as a set of humane portraits, not a textbook.

Open Library note. Music can move us to the heights or depths of emotion. It can persuade us to buy something, or remind us of our first date. It can lift us out of depression when nothing else can. It can get us dancing to its beat. But the power of music goes much, much further. Indeed, music occupies more areas of our brain than language does–humans are a musical species.
