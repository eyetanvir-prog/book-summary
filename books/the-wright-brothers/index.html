The Wright Brothers

Two bicycle makers from Ohio who solved powered flight with patience, method, and very little money.

David McCulloughFirst published 2015About 432 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The Wrights succeeded by focusing on control, not just power, studying birds and testing gliders before ever adding an engine.

  2. 02

    Their method was relentlessly empirical: when published data proved wrong, they built a wind tunnel and generated their own.

  3. 03

    McCullough tells an admiring, gentle story and largely skips the brothers’ later patent battles and harder-edged business conduct.

Open Library note. Two-time winner of the Pulitzer Prize David McCullough tells the dramatic story of the courageous brothers who taught the world how to fly. On a winter day in 1903, on the remote Outer Banks of North Carolina, two unknown brothers from Ohio, Wilbur and Orville Wright, changed history. The age of flight had begun with the first heavier-than-air powered machine carrying a pilot.
