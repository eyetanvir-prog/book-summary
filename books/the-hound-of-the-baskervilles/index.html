The Hound of the Baskervilles

A family curse, a spectral dog, and a lonely moor give Sherlock Holmes his most atmospheric case.

Arthur Conan DoyleFirst published 1900About 207 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Doyle pits superstition against reason, letting the moor feel genuinely uncanny before Holmes insists on evidence over legend.

  2. 02

    Watson does much of the legwork here, and the novel shows how much a careful, honest observer contributes even without Holmes’s brilliance.

  3. 03

    The landscape is a character: fog, bogs, and prehistoric huts create dread more effectively than any single scene of violence.

Open Library note. The Hound of the Baskervilles is the third of the four crime novels by British writer Arthur Conan Doyle featuring the detective Sherlock Holmes. Originally serialised in The Strand Magazine from August 1901 to April 1902, it is set in 1889 largely on Dartmoor in Devon in England's West Country and tells the story of an attempted murder inspired by the legend of a fearsome, diabolical hound of supernatural origin. Holmes and Watson investigate the case.
