Society of the Spectacle

Guy Debord argues that modern life has turned into images, with social relationships mediated by spectacle.

Guy DebordFirst published 1967About 157 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    The spectacle, Debord says, is not just media but a social relationship among people mediated by images.

  2. 02

    Consumer capitalism, in his account, turns living into watching, replacing direct experience with its representation.

  3. 03

    Written as short numbered theses, the book is cryptic but influential for anyone thinking about media and politics.

Open Library note. Few works of political and cultural theory have been as enduringly provocative as Guy Debord's The Society of the Spectacle. From its publication amid the social upheavals of the 1960s up to the present, the volatile theses of this book have decisively transformed debates on the shape of modernity, capitalism and everyday life in the late twentieth cenlury.
