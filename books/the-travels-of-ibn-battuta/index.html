The Travels of Ibn Battuta

The fourteenth-century Moroccan scholar’s account of nearly three decades of travel across the Muslim world and beyond.

Ibn BattutaFirst published 1829About 397 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Ibn Battuta’s travels from North Africa to India, China, and West Africa show how a shared faith and law linked a vast, diverse world.

  2. 02

    As a trained jurist, he found work and hospitality wherever Islamic courts existed, revealing how scholarly networks functioned like a passport.

  3. 03

    The narrative was dictated years later and includes borrowed or embellished passages, so historians read it as a valuable but imperfect source.
