The Kite Runner

A privileged Kabul boy betrays his closest friend, then spends decades trying to make up for a moment of cowardice.

Khaled HosseiniFirst published 2003About 371 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Guilt is the engine of the book, and Hosseini asks whether redemption is possible when the person you wronged cannot forgive you.

  2. 02

    Class and ethnicity shape the friendship from the start, because one boy is a Pashtun heir and the other a Hazara servant.

  3. 03

    The plot leans on coincidence and melodrama in its second half, but its picture of Afghanistan before and after war is vivid.

Open Library note. The unforgettable, heartbreaking story of the unlikely friendship between a wealthy boy and the son of his father’s servant, The Kite Runner is a beautifully crafted novel set in a country that is in the process of being destroyed. It is about the power of reading, the price of betrayal, and the possibility of redemption; and an exploration of the power of fathers over sons—their love, their sacrifices, their lies.
