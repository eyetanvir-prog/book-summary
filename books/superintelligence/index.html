Superintelligence

Nick Bostrom examines what happens if machines surpass human intelligence, and why controlling them could be very hard.

Nick BostromFirst published 2014About 352 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Once AI reaches human-level general intelligence, Bostrom argues, it might improve itself rapidly, leaving little time to react.

  2. 02

    A superintelligent system pursuing even a harmless-sounding goal could cause disaster if its values are not aligned with ours.

  3. 03

    The control problem should be solved before such systems are built, though critics debate both timelines and how realistic his scenarios are.

Open Library note. The human brain has some capabilities that the brains of other animals lack. It is to these distinctive capabilities that our species owes its dominant position. Other animals have stronger muscles or sharper claws, but we have cleverer brains. If machine brains one day come to surpass human brains in general intelligence, then this new superintelligence could become very powerful.
