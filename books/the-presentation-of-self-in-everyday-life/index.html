The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life

Erving Goffman treats social life as theatre, showing how people perform roles and manage impressions every day.

Erving GoffmanFirst published 1952About 255 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    People present a “front” in social situations, controlling appearance and manner to shape how others see them.

  2. 02

    There is a front stage and a backstage, and much behaviour depends on which region someone believes they are in.

  3. 03

    Teams cooperate to maintain performances, so social order relies on people helping each other avoid embarrassment.

Open Library note. A notable contribution to our understanding of ourselves. This book explores the realm of human behavior in social situations and the way that we appear to others. Dr. Goffman uses the metaphor of theatrical performance as a framework. Each person in everyday social intercourse presents himself and his activity to others, attempts to guide and control the impressions they form of him, and employs certain techniques in order to sustain his performance, just as an actor presents a character to an audience.
