The Castle

Kafka’s unfinished novel follows a land surveyor trying to reach an authority that keeps moving out of reach.

Franz KafkaFirst published 1926About 384 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Bureaucracy becomes a maze where rules, officials, and permissions seem real but never become fully understandable.

  2. 02

    K.’s struggle reflects alienation: wanting acceptance from a system that offers no clear reason to grant or deny it.

  3. 03

    Kafka’s dry humor makes the frustration recognizable, especially for anyone who has fought an institution that will not answer.

Open Library note. The Castle (original title: "Das Schloß") is the story of K., the unwanted Land Surveyor who is never to be admitted to the Castle nor accepted in the village, and yet cannot go home. As he encounters dualities of certainty and doubt, hope and fear, and reason and nonsense, K.'s struggles in the absurd, labyrinthine world where he finds himself seem to reveal an inexplicable truth about the nature of existence.
