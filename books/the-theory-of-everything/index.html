The Theory of Everything

Hawking’s short lecture tour from Aristotle to black holes, aimed at readers who want the big picture without equations.

Stephen HawkingFirst published 2002About 132 pagesCard · 1 min

Three ideas to keep

  1. 01

    Each era’s cosmology was a model that fit the evidence until better observations broke it, and Hawking treats today’s physics the same way.

  2. 02

    Black holes are not perfectly black: quantum effects let them radiate slowly, linking gravity, heat, and quantum theory in one surprising result.

  3. 03

    A unified theory joining general relativity and quantum mechanics is the open goal; the book explains why it is hard rather than claiming victory.
